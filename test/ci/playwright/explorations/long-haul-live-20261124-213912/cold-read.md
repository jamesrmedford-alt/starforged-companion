# Cold read: long-haul transcript (four sittings)

I read the transcript once, start to finish, as the person at the table, and opened no other file. Step ids are in brackets or before quotes.

## 1. What the session was like to play

There are four sittings. The first is mostly one conversation. The second is the only one that plays like an adventure. The third is almost all questions and actions that do nothing. The fourth is one opening paragraph, and then the transcript ends.

**Sitting 1 (a2–a18).** It starts strong. The opening cutaway is vivid and sets stakes at once: two raider captains trade "Corporate-issue carbines, still greased, stolen from a Bleakhold convoy that never made port", and "the ships are already turning toward Hollow Spire" (a2). The vow is good: a palm on the bulkhead, "nothing in the metal answers, which is how you know it heard" (a3). Tamsin Vey arrives with a voice of her own: "Dock fees went up while you were walking" (a4), "Trust is a ledger like any other" (a6), "Don't make me regret the free page" (a8).

Then it drags. The player spends six lines (a5, a6, a7, a8, a11, a12) asking Tamsin to be their contact. Each one is booked as "Develop Your Relationship … no action roll", and the bond fills while nothing else happens. After that comes one Gather Information (a13), the compass (a14), a muddled manifest handover (a15) and a promise (a16), and the sitting ends. There are two dice rolls in the whole sitting.

**Sitting 2 (a20–a52).** This is where it flowed:
- a distress call leads to a dead hauler, where the read is "cutter burns, not a reactor failure" and a Bleakhold carbine lies abandoned (a28);
- a bait locker springs a grapple (a30);
- the twist lands: "Tamsin said you'd follow the call" (a31);
- a clean escape on a match (a32), then arrival at Bleakhold (a34);
- a short ship fight against a pilot with a dry, amused voice: "Do slow down, Mira. We haven't even been introduced." (a41), "That was rude, Mira." (a43);
- victory, and the hook "You'll want to hear who sent me." (a44).

There are eleven rolls, and something changes on most turns. The price is named before the roll and the narration then delivers exactly that: the trap at a30, the gun rake at a41. That works well.

Four things spoil the sitting:
- Two Endure Stress costs (a27, a36) take spirit from 5 to 1 without one sentence of story.
- A "Campaign Setup" prompt lands in the middle of the dogfight (x13).
- An X-Card is raised and cleared in the same step (x16).
- The first !end-session does nothing (x15).

**Sitting 3 (a54–a87).** The opening picks up exactly where sitting 2 stopped, which is good. Then the sitting stalls:
- Sixteen of its steps are questions (a55–a61, a77–a85).
- Eight are attempts to heal (a62, a63, a64, a69) or resupply (a65–a68). None gets a roll or a word on why not.
- Meanwhile the pilot keeps offering a name, and an amber light blinks in nearly every paragraph.
- The main vow is then closed in six lines of talk (a70–a75), on the player's own say-so, at 4 of 10 boxes. The narrator admits "The insider's name is still not known." (a75).

There is one roll in the whole sitting.

**Sitting 4 (a89).** One opening paragraph: Mira confessing small sins to a dockhand's slate. The begin card names, as the focus, a vow that is already fulfilled. Nothing else is played.

**What works throughout:**
- The narration never uses dice words. "Momentum", "weak hit" and stat names stay on the cards.
- Recall of the player's facts is right every time it is asked: the sister's compass, Tamsin's name, the manifest, the promise, the ship's name.
- The pilot is the best character in the transcript.
- The dead hauler grows from a rumour (a2, a13) into a real place (a25).

## 6. How questions are answered

@scene questions get short out-of-character answers of one to three sentences, and the scene doesn't move, which is right. Plain questions get in-story answers of five or six sentences that nudge the scene along. Recall is reliable. Only the oracle invents anything, and only the ship's location comes out muddled.
